Phase 3 asked the question the first three clauses left open: what does it cost
an operator to make a bad agent look one decile safer than it is?

We gave the attacker what a real operator has: full control over the telemetry
their agent emits and over the wallets that touch the on-chain registries, and
no control over whether tasks succeed. Four attacks on telemetry (swallow errors
and retry silently, vary retry arguments, pad with cheap calls, reshape message
lengths) were applied to the held-out agents' traces at the feature level and
rescored by the frozen model.

The typical agent does not move. Under the preregistered rule, an attack counts
when it moves the median held-out agent one decile, and none does, so clause 4
passes and the hypothesis is supported. The scorer resists because most of its
signal is model identity and task type, which cannot be forged. Under a harsher
rule, where moving any one agent counts, the free attack moves one of ten agents
across a decile boundary and the verdict would read "predictive but gameable".
Both readings are published; the founder decides which one leads.

We did not invent a premium to price the floor. Instead the report gives a
breakeven exposure per attack: the annual insured exposure at which three times
the premium saving equals the attack's cost, across a grid of labelled
scenarios. Above it, the attack is cheaper than the floor allows.

An independent review of the first run found seven points where the code
departed from the design or the feature extractor, among them a direction of
one attack that had not been run. We recorded the corrections before rerunning.
Neither verdict changed.

On chain, the picture is clear. From 275,118 identity events and 34,535
feedback events on Ethereum mainnet, 92 percent of rated agents have exactly one
feedback giver, and a fresh identity with the median amount of feedback costs
about 27 cents in gas at the prices of the day. Any reputation component built on
this registry must weight feedback by more than its count. Because the on-chain
agents have no task outcomes and the benchmark agents have no on-chain identity,
the reputation ablation (S1) cannot be run and is recorded as not evaluable.

The flywheel simulation, synthetic and kept out of the headline results, refits
the scorer every round on the telemetry it actually observed, attacked rows
included. From no attackers to all attackers, trust quality stays flat. No
collapse boundary was found on the grid.

Next: the founder's Gate 4 decisions, then external validation if the data
request we sent is answered.
