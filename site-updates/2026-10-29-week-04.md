Phase 4 was meant to test the failure model on data we did not collect
ourselves. No such data exists: the one dataset that pairs agents with task
outcomes has no public release and its authors have not replied, and the public
ERC-8004 sources hold identities and feedback, not outcomes. So we did the two
things that were still honest to do.

First, we checked our own Ethereum collection against an independent one. A
CC0 dataset on Harvard Dataverse covers the first 10,000 ERC-8004 agents on
Ethereum: mint blocks, transaction hashes, owners, transfers and feedback
records. We compared it with our logs row for row, with no tolerance. Every one
of the 10,000 agents agrees on mint block, transaction hash and owner. Every
one of their 1,961 transfer rows and 980 feedback rows is in our logs, and
nothing in their window is in ours that is not in theirs. Where their feedback
counts differ from ours, ours are larger, because our fetch is three months
later. The one thing we had to infer is the block at which they read owners;
the data does not say, and the smallest block at which all 10,000 owners agree
is 24,744,960. The sentence we wanted to write is "our collection matches the
public dataset row for row", and the data lets us write it.

Second, we tried to extend the on-chain reputation picture to Base and BNB
Smart Chain. The free endpoints we could use without a key or a sign-up serve
anonymous clients only their last few days of history, about three on BNB and
46 on Base, so a full-history fetch was not possible tonight. We fetched the
two days before each chain's head and cut the same two days from the end of
our Ethereum file. In that window, Ethereum's 2,571 feedback entries came from
a single wallet writing to 792 agents. Base's 2,161 entries came from 2,098
different wallets, each writing to about one agent. BNB registered 3,506
agents and received nine feedback entries. The concentration we described in
week three is Ethereum's pattern; whether Base's holds over its full history
is an open question we have labelled as such.

A one-request check against the 8004scan public API bounds our counts: on
Ethereum our 55,407 registrations are exactly ids 0 to 55,406, below the newest
id the API reported four days later. Everything fetched is in the manifest with
a checksum and a licence, and every rule was written down before the run.

The public research page now shows the results so far, which phases are done,
what exists in the private repository for anyone who wants to verify, and the
flywheel question marked answered within the study's limits. What remains is
the weekly reports and the write-up due on day 90.
