title: Week two, first results
status: in-progress
question: primary
summary: On held-out agents, telemetry lifts failure-prediction AUC from 0.65 to 0.84 and the first three of four preregistered clauses hold. The gain is concentrated in one benchmark, portability across domains is not supported, and the gameability clause is untested.
---
Phase 2 ran faster than planned. The held-out split was frozen before any
model was fit, features were extracted from raw traces, and the preregistered
evaluation ran on 10 held-out agents, 9,298 traces.

The telemetry model's AUC is 0.84 (95 percent interval 0.78 to 0.89) against
0.65 for the naive baseline that knows only the model, the task type and the
task length. Brier skill is 0.22 with an interval excluding zero. Traces in the
top predicted-risk quartile fail 4.1 times as often as the bottom quartile, and
failure rises monotonically across every decile. Those are clauses 1 to 3 of
the preregistration, and all three hold. A five-fold cross-validation over all
41 agents gives the same picture.

Two things stop us from calling H1 supported. First, clause 4, the cost of
gaming the score, is Phase 3 work and has not been run. Second, when we score
inside each benchmark separately, almost all of the gain comes from AgentDojo,
which is 82 percent of our rows. Inside SWE-bench telemetry does no better
than the baseline. Inside tau-bench and WebArena the gain is small and its
interval touches zero. The preregistered test is pooled and it passes; the
broader claim that telemetry predicts failure across task domains is not yet
established, and we are saying so.

The portability test fails outright: a score trained on other benchmarks does
not beat a source's own naive baseline in any of the four. Two of those sources
have only one held-out agent, so the intervals there are not informative.

Next is Gate 3, where the founder reviews these results and sets the dollar
floor that clause 4 needs before the adversarial phase can start. Every number
above, with intervals and per-source tables, is in the results package in the
repository.
